# Branded Best — Cold Call Cheat Sheet

Talking points, not a script. Say it your way.
**Who you're calling:** the owner at small Arizona companies, the HR / people manager at bigger ones.

## Rule #1 — skip the long intro
Get their first name, say who you are in one line, then go straight to *why you're calling for them*.
> Cutting the self-intro and leading with the prospect's problem took him from worst to best rep in about a week.
> — Sabri Suby, "18 Years of Copywriting Advice in 93 Minutes"

## Openers (pick one)
- **Owner:** "Hey [Name], it's Cole with Branded Best here in [AZ city]. Quick one: when you hand out shirts or gifts to your team or customers, who handles that for you right now?"
- **HR:** "Hey [Name], Cole with Branded Best, local here in Arizona. I work with HR folks on onboarding kits and work-anniversary gifts. What are you doing for new hires right now?"

## Talking points (bring up whatever fits)
1. **Stuff people actually use.** Logo tumblers, good shirts, hats. Not junk that ends up in a drawer. Used daily = your logo seen daily.
   *(Max Sturtevant: give a gift tied to real use, like a tumbler, not throwaway swag)*
2. **Built for Arizona.** A tumbler that keeps ice for hours in 110° heat is a gift people keep.
3. **Local.** We're in Arizona: easy to reach, no guessing with an overseas website. `[fill in: turnaround time]`
4. **HR moments:** new-hire welcome kits, work anniversaries, holiday gifts, team events, hydration/wellness pushes.
5. **Owner moments:** crew shirts/uniforms, client thank-you gifts, trade shows, referral thank-yous.
6. **Easy to order.** `[fill in: minimum order? free logo mockup? price range per tumbler?]`
7. **Proof.** `[fill in: 1–2 local companies we've done work for]`

## Questions that keep them talking
- "What did you give out last time? How'd people like it?"
- "Any new hires or team events coming up in the next couple months?"
- "Who besides you weighs in on stuff like this?"

## Quick answers to pushback
- **"Just send me some info."** → "Happy to. So I send the right thing, is this more for employees or customers?" Then offer a free logo mockup.
- **"We already have someone."** → "Totally fine. Lots of folks keep us as a backup for rush jobs or tumblers. Mind if I send a mockup with your logo to compare?"
- **"No budget / not right now."** → "When's your next new-hire class or holiday push? I'll call you back then."
- **Gatekeeper blocks you.** → Be friendly and make them look good to the boss ("this makes the new-hire gifts easy on you"). If it's a dead end, find another way to reach the owner.
  *(Sweat Equity: the screener decides if you reach the boss; Open Residency: don't quit when a gatekeeper shuts you down)*

## Don't
Don't describe them as doing something embarrassing ("Tired of cheap swag?"). People deny it and tune out.
*(Daniel Fazio, "A Step By Step Guide To Screwing Up An Amazing Offer")*

## Every call ends with a next step
Free logo mockup · sample drop-off · or a set call-back date.

## After the call
- Send a short email the same day. Calls and email back each other up. *(Instantly, "Top 4 Signals For Cold Email Campaigns")*
- Write down every objection you hear. The ones that repeat tell us what to fix in the offer. *(Daniel Iles, "$100K/m VS $1M/m offers")*
